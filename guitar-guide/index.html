The quest

Week by week

Open a week to see its five practice days. Days 6 and 7 are rest days. If a day feels hard, repeat it tomorrow. Getting it right matters more than staying on schedule.

Every practice day

The 15-minute routine

2 min

Tune & wiggle

Tune with the tuner below. Wiggle fingers, shake hands out, sit tall.

3 min

Warm-up

Pluck the string rainbow, then a spider walk (frets 1-2-3-4) on one string.

6 min

Today's job

The one new thing from the weekly plan. Slow and steady.

3 min

Play a song

Something she already knows. This part should feel easy and fun.

1 min

Star time

Tick the day, say one thing she did well, and put the guitar away together.

Chord charts

The eight chords

Read each chart like the guitar standing up and facing you. The thickest string is on the left. Numbers in dots tell which finger to use: 1 pointer, 2 middle, 3 ring. O means play the string open. X means skip it. Press Hear it to listen.

Songbook

Songs to learn

Melody songs use tab. Each line is a string, and the number shows which fret to press (0 means open). Chord songs show the chord above the word where it changes. Strum four times on each chord unless it says otherwise.

Practice helpers

Tuner, beat-keeper and switch game

String rainbow tuner

Tap a string to hear its note, then turn the peg until the guitar sounds the same. A clip-on tuner is easier for a parent, and this works when one isn't nearby.

Beat-keeper

Strum once each time a circle lights up. Start at 60 and go faster only when it feels easy.

For grown-ups

How to coach a five-year-old

The right guitar

  • Size: most 5-year-olds fit a 1/4 size (about 30–31 inches). A tall five may fit a 1/2 size. Her feet should rest flat and her arm should reach over the body comfortably.
  • Nylon strings are much softer on small fingers than steel strings.
  • A clip-on tuner and some colored dot stickers for the frets used in week 2.
  • A footstool or a low chair so her knees sit level.

Keep it fun

  • Stop while she still wants to play. Ten happy minutes beat thirty tired ones.
  • Practice at the same time every day, for example right after breakfast.
  • Praise effort ("you kept the beat!") more than results.
  • Sing together. Singing while strumming builds rhythm faster than anything else.
  • Let her be the teacher sometimes. Explaining a chord to a parent helps her remember it.

Normal at this age

  • Fingertips get a little sore in weeks 2–4. Short sessions let them toughen up.
  • Buzzing strings mean she needs to press closer to the fret wire.
  • Wanting to quit some days. Skip a day, play a fun song, come back.
  • Full-size G and C chords are a stretch for small hands. That is why this plan uses one-finger versions first.

Videos to watch together

These open a YouTube search in a new tab. Pick a short, friendly video and watch it together before trying the skill. Grown-ups should preview videos first.

Beginner to expert

After the three months

What to expect by Week 12: a confident beginner who can tune with help, keep a steady beat, play 6 chords, switch between three of them in time, and perform several songs. Getting to an expert level takes years of steady practice for anyone. The roadmap below shows where to go next.

StageWhenWhat she learns
Guitar StarMonths 1–3This plan: parts of the guitar, beat, simple melodies, 6–8 chords, first concert.
Song BuilderMonths 4–6Full G, C and D chords, E and A major, smooth changes at 80 beats, two strumming patterns, reading simple tab.
Rhythm HeroMonths 7–12Fingerpicking patterns, first power chords, the notes on the low strings, playing with other people.
MusicianYears 2–3Barre chords (once hands are bigger), reading music, scales, simple solos, a full-size or 3/4 guitar.
AdvancedYear 4 and upImprovising, music theory, a chosen style (classical, rock, folk), lessons with a teacher.
